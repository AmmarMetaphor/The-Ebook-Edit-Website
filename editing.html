Which level do you need?

Compare the editing levels at a glance.

Each level solves a different kind of problem. Matching the level to the manuscript protects both quality and budget.

Proofreading is the final quality check and is not a substitute for developmental editing, line editing or copy editing where those stages are required.

01

Editorial assessment

A diagnostic review that identifies the manuscript’s strengths, weaknesses, audience fit, and recommended next steps.

  • Structure and chapter logic
  • Clarity and reader experience
  • Voice and positioning
  • Priority revision plan
6
02

Developmental editing

Big-picture work on argument, story, sequence, pacing, repetition, gaps, and the overall architecture of the book.

  • Chapter-level comments
  • Structural recommendations
  • Content additions or cuts
  • Revision roadmap
7
03

Line and copy editing

Sentence-level refinement for readability, tone, grammar, syntax, word choice, consistency, and flow.

  • Tracked changes
  • Editorial queries
  • Style sheet
  • Consistency checks
8
04

Proofreading

A final quality check after layout, focused on remaining errors rather than major rewrites.

  • Typos and punctuation
  • Formatting consistency
  • Headings and navigation
  • Final file review
9

A useful distinction

Editing is not one single pass.

Structural issues should be solved before sentence polishing, and sentence polishing should be completed before final proofreading.

Skipping that sequence often means paying to perfect paragraphs that later need to be rewritten or removed.
